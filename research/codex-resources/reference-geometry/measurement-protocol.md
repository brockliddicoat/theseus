# closing the two mechanical reference gaps

no physical measurement was performed in this lane. `measurements-template.csv` has only a header; all actual dimensions stay null in `mechanical-reference-gaps.json`.

for a useful competitor click comparison, record the exact model, revision, sample identity and assembly state. use several independently acquired production units if making a population claim; a single unit supports only that sample. measure the left and right buttons separately at the front, middle and rear of each guiding contact. photograph the intact arrangement and retain the source photo privately with the specimen record.

measure **lateral guide clearance** separately from **vertical plunger pretravel**. a POM pad under a plunger can tune the vertical stack without changing a lateral rail gap. identify the pad material from a maker drawing or reliable part identification before calling it POM. measure slot width, tongue width, each relevant pad thickness, and the contact positions with a calibrated method suitable for the feature size. report instrument resolution, calibration uncertainty, temperature and repeatability alongside the result; displayed decimal places alone do not establish accuracy.

for a simple two-sided guide, the dimensional model is `c = W_slot - w_tongue - t_left - t_right`. worst-case bounds are `c_min = W_min - w_max - t_left,max - t_right,max` and `c_max = W_max - w_min - t_left,min - t_right,min`. these are engineering stack equations, not measurements of an existing mouse. omit a pad term if the actual architecture has no pad on that contact. a designed elastic preload needs its own force/deflection model.

a dial-indicator reversal test at a documented small lateral force gives **apparent movement**: `x(+F) - x(-F)`. it includes elastic compliance and fixture motion as well as lost motion. retain that as a separate measurement; do not report it as pure clearance. measure vertical free travel and switch actuation separately. a force/displacement curve before and after replacing a pad can show the tuning effect without inventing a competitor's manufacturing tolerance. repeat each setup after reseating the fixture, retaining all readings.

for the G-Wolves reinforcement reference, first recover the two original F28.3 attachments or a directly identified intact specimen of the same model. the missing personal photos have not been seen by this lane. take a top-down board photo with a scale in the board plane and a side view for thickness; use at least two independent known distances to assess scale and perspective. trace the complete board, arm roots, contact ends and mounting datums as separate editable paths. preserve observed fractures on their own layer. label any hidden boundary or inferred continuation as estimated. do not scale a narrow arm from the external mouse width alone.

geometry and breakage are different evidence. a photo can establish visible shape and the location of an observed break; it cannot establish a failure load or shipping failure rate. a mechanical comparison of small arms against a whole-board load path needs the actual section geometry, FR4 layup and thickness, support/contact conditions and load direction. retain the important whole-board concept while keeping an unverified numerical advantage out of the reference asset.
