## Direct Drive: exactly what “6% sooner” can mean

let `d` be equal switch-axis travel and `v` the same prescribed trigger-path speed. at a locally fixed misalignment θ:

```text
v_axis_before = v cos θ
t_before = d / (v cos θ)
t_after = d / v
t_after / t_before = cos θ
sooner = 1 − cos 20° = 6.0307%
```

an illustrative `d=0.35mm`, `v=0.1mm/ms` gives `3.7246ms → 3.5000ms` for that motion segment. these are chosen inputs, not measured click times. any fixed electronic delay reduces the percentage gain in total end-to-end time. the actual contact/NC event's travel and trigger arc are unmeasured here. both axes should use the same actuation criterion and preload.

an alternative force-driven model starts from rest with equal effective mass, equal axial travel and no resistance. projecting a constant force gives `t_after/t_before = sqrt(cos20°)`, or **3.0623% sooner**. with constant axial resistance `R`, the ratio becomes `sqrt((Fcos20°−R)/(F−R))`, only while both cases have positive net force. at `R/F=0.9`, that toy model gives 37.0%; at `0.95` the misaligned case cannot start. these are sensitivity examples showing why a projection cannot fix real time, not additional product claims. source for operating-axis intent: [Omron basic-switch guide](https://www.ia.omron.com/data_pdf/guide/29/microswitch_tg_e_3_2.pdf).

**recommended overlay:** “94% → 100% along the axis” + “6% sooner · ideal travel, same press speed.” the large “6% sooner” can remain approved wording, but its assumptions need legible placement. a force arrow and a velocity arrow must be distinguished; one must not be silently substituted for the other. `sinθ` and `cosθ` are perpendicular components; their scalar percentages do not add, and side force by itself is not dissipated energy.

**to promote to a product result:** a matched jig must record trigger travel/velocity, contact force, preload, switch electrical edge and USB event, for both mechanisms and multiple press positions/speeds. report electrical latency separately from the mechanics. no geometry ratio should become a measured player advantage.

## optical: the arithmetic survives, the attribution does not

the [D2FP test conditions](https://www.fa.omron.com.cn/data_pdf/cat/cdva-cn1-002a.pdf?id=3906) support a **hypothetical** rectangular-pulse average: `5mA ×30/1000 =0.15mA` per switch, `0.30mA` for two. test pulses do not establish the pulse schedule of Razer's selected switch and firmware.

the inherited `306mAh /180h =1.70mA` is an implied battery-side average, with a capacity inherited from a secondary review and hours from a maker claim. dividing these different-rail currents produces the familiar 17.65%, then `1/(1−0.1765)−1 =21.43%`; those percentages do **not** follow physically until voltages, conversion and complete circuit loads are known. [Razer's primary page](https://www.razer.com/newsroom/product-news/viper-v4-pro-arrives-in-esports-green) does not provide that breakdown.

the script makes the distinction explicit. with **assumed** 3.7V battery and the same hypothetical 1.70mA baseline, baseline power is 6.29mW. at an **assumed** 1.8V switch rail and 90% conversion, the two pulsed emitters draw 0.54mW on that rail, equivalent to **0.60mW from the battery**: 9.54% share and 10.54% ideal runtime gain if completely removed. choosing a **different assumed** 3.3V rail gives 0.99mW on the rail, equivalent to **1.10mW from the battery**: 17.49% /21.19%. neither is a Razer result. the exercise shows that source currents alone cannot select an answer. photoreceiver, drive, switch scanning, Hall, MCU, regulator and pullup power must all be included in the replacement budget.

## Hall: preserve the wake behavior while saving energy

[TMAG5253 Rev E](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tmag5253.pdf) is an example sensor, not a chosen encoder. its table estimates 90.5µA for 50µs enabled plus **1ms sleep**. the simple 2mA rectangular model instead gives 95.26µA, including a chosen 20nA sleep term. the table value is numerically compatible with roughly 1.9mA pulses; this does not identify the variant. enabled time includes startup, settling and sampling, so 50µs is not automatically 50µs of valid data. two sensors double sensor current before the host is counted.

[TMAG3001 Rev C](https://www.ti.com/lit/gpn/TMAG3001), p5, supplies a sharper warning about wake intervals: its long-interval example is 0.4µA at 1.8V or 0.5µA at 3.3V with a **5-second** wake interval. at **1ms**, 3.3V, one magnetic channel and no averaging, the table gives **110µA typical /250µA max**; four channels give **230/375µA**. standby's 0.45**mA** is a different state. the homepage's 0.45µA wake/sleep headline cannot be used as an always-ready encoder budget.

the generated JSON includes an explicit energy example: two 2mA sensors at 1.8V, active for a chosen 8 hours plus the simple 1ms-sleep duty model for a chosen 16 hours. it reports sensor supply energy and battery energy at a chosen 90% conversion. those durations and circuits are illustrations; no mouse endurance is calculated.

the decision needed before procurement is the encoder topology and wake policy. while playing, use a validated conversion interval; while idle, keep a timer or autonomous magnetic interrupt capable of noticing the first scroll. detect scroll-only wake on a stationary mouse, rearm wake mode correctly, preserve direction and count the initial notch. a long sleep can miss motion that begins and reverses before sampling. for an **invented** 24-detent wheel, four quadrature transitions per detent and 10rev/s, transition dwell is 1.0417ms, already shorter than a 1.05ms cycle. absolute-angle decoding has different wrapping limits. this is a design example, not a Theseus wheel specification.

## thin walls, materials and shape

[MIT beam displacement](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-11-mechanics-of-materials-fall-1999/13534abb67b86d2fb8ee11a1f4f2b8c4_MIT3_11F99_bdisp.pdf) and [beam stress](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-11-mechanics-of-materials-fall-1999/96d839b02e4a6c63cf8031800e89cccd_MIT3_11F99_bstress.pdf) support the model equations:

```text
I = b h³/12                         delta = P L³/(48 E I)
M_max = P L/4                       sigma_max = M_max h/(2I)
mass = rho L b h                    [consistent units]
```

for fixed length, width and equal mass, `h ∝1/ρ`, so bending stiffness scales with `E/ρ³`. maximizing `E⅓/ρ` finds minimum mass at a specified stiffness. it is an index, not a stiffness unit. an isotropic plate adds Poisson's ratio: `[E/(1−ν²)]⅓/ρ`. a curved shell can carry membrane loads and needs its real supports/curvature; an orthotropic laminate needs directional stiffness rather than a scalar plate substitution.

for equal mass/footprint and a common bending failure criterion, moment capacity scales with `σ/ρ²`, so its minimum-mass index is `sqrt(σ)/ρ`. an axial tie uses `E/ρ` for stiffness and `σ/ρ` for strength. if width **and** depth are allowed to scale at fixed aspect ratio, the bending stiffness index changes to `sqrt(E)/ρ`. “better for its weight” needs the intended job and free dimension.

using the inherited PP-C-20A /AZ91D scalar values, the PP wall is 1.792× thicker at equal mass, with a 1.201× bending-mass index and **1.734× calculated fixed-width bending stiffness**. its inherited nominal tensile-strength/mass ratio is 0.935× magnesium's. these statements can all be true together; they are not measured shell rankings. the [official Mitsubishi table](https://www.m-chemical.co.jp/carbon-fiber/pdf/pellet/pellet.pdf) now confirms PP-C-20A density1.01g/cm³, flexural modulus13.5GPa and tensile120MPa. it gives **210MPa flexural strength**, whereas the inherited PlaBase text says207MPa. retain the source-specific difference; do not quietly replace tensile120 with flexural210 in the existing failure study.

the standalone JSON reproduces all six inherited input rows to make the arithmetic reviewable. the metals/ABS source values were not fully primary-reverified in this pass; their rows say so. the source set also mixes tensile yield, tensile ultimate and flexural/tensile modulus, so use it for conditional teaching arithmetic, not a universal “strongest material” ranking. [Gund's FR4 sheet](https://thegundcompany.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NEMA-FR4-Glass-Epoxy-Laminate-by-The-Gund-Co.pdf) is directional typical coupon data; a real populated PCB is not that coupon.

the revamp's 60mm-span, 10×3.3mm PP-C-20A beam weighs **1.9998g**. at the chosen 10N center load, its ideal deflection is **0.11131mm**, compared with **0.01212mm** after rotating the same section: **9.1827× bending stiffness**. equal-stress load capacity grows **3.0303×**, not9.1827×. the deep orientation has `L/h=6`, so shear deformation and three-dimensional effects should be checked before turning the Euler–Bernoulli number into a precise physical prediction. the force is a stated example, not a measured hand-load distribution. halving depth halves mass but reduces this bending stiffness to one eighth and bending capacity at equal stress to one quarter. ±10% depth gives −27.1% /+33.1% stiffness in the ideal equation.

the triangle explanation is strongest as a classroom racking demonstration, followed by the actual stated lattice-model result. the [Ashby paper](https://eclass.aegean.gr/modules/document/file.php/511165/projects/foams_lattices.pdf) supports a conditional distinction between stretch-dominated and bending-dominated structures and explicitly treats buckling as another limit. it does not prove that arbitrary mouse-shell loads always produce stretching, that a thinner triangle cannot buckle, or that lower deflection proves higher failure strength.

for a free planar pin-jointed frame, the full count is `b−2j+3 = s−m`, where `s` counts independent self-stress states and `m` internal infinitesimal mechanisms. an isostatic frame has both zero. a zero left side alone can also hide equal nonzero `s` and `m`, so it does not prove rigidity. a rigid redundant frame can have `b>2j−3`. supports and three-dimensional geometry require their corresponding constraint counts.

## reproducibility and handoff boundaries
