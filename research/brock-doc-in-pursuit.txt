2) Rotating the microswitches to be aligned with the angle of the triggers.
Endgame Gear uses this implementation in their mice, and they were voted “Best Click Feeling” during the Peripheral Awards. This setup prevents your input force from being wasted by possibly being split into separate directional vectors. Having your input force directly driven into the microswitch not only tactically feels better but also reduces post travel. Furthermore, the result of all this is better mechanical latency, leading to better player performance. There is an opportunity for this to be marketed as “Direct Drive Actuation”.

3) A dual trigger plungers implementation.
Popularized by G-Wolves, this is the method of adding an additional plunger as far away from the first one as possible to provide a second anchor point for each trigger. It was created to resolve the issue of horizontal trigger jiggle/sway, so it should result in better alignment and more consistent clicks (which leads to more consistent performance). This could be marketed as “Dual Rail Triggers”.


4) True dual/split click tensioning.
So many mice are held back from being perfect by their click tensioning mechanisms. For example on even a leadership performance mouse such as the ULX, the triggers on it do not each have their own tensioning mechanisms and instead are both mounted by the same flat, single-screw centralized click tensioning amalgamation. With this system, a tiny misalignment of how the triggers are mounted can lead to a completely compromised and uneven feeling of click tensioning. A dual/split click tensioning implementation could theoretically vastly improve the click tensioning quality control which may lead to less muscle strain and more consistent click timing (therefore theoretically increasing accuracy as well). This upgrade was made from the Arye FCC-1 to RCC-1 and it led to a massive upgrade in click feeling.


7) Switching the scroll wheel axle to POM.
This allows for every step of every scroll rotation to be self-lubricating, significantly reducing the risk of the scroll wheel becoming “sticky” after prolonged usage. Arye originally theorized the usefulness of a POM scroll wheel for longevity, and PMM shortly followed suit by implementing this feature on their higher-end models. At this point there are years of customer reception to support that there are only upsides for this type of axel.


8) POM mouse skates.
Due to POM mouse skates being pioneered by Artisan, they should be perceived as more premium and of having smoother glide ability (which means more consistent performance). This also introduces material uniformity throughout the mouse. POM skate should be installed by default, but PTFE skates should still be included for those with preference.


10) Usage of 2 dot skates per corner by default.
Many skate designs bend irreparably when removed or realigned. Additionally, most skates are just designed based on what area is available on the bottom of a mouse, which can lead to uneven friction distribution because of the resulting limited axes of symmetry. Because of their circular nature (circles having infinite axes of symmetry), dot skates do not have any of these issues (and are even lighter). As a precaution though, there should be enough room for 2 dot skates per corner as with only 1 dot per corner some softer pads cause the dots to sink in too much and the pad to scrape the bottom of the mouse.


11) Not having cutouts under the skates.
	This prevents dust from getting on the adhesive of the skates. It also gives the skates more surface area to stick to so they can stick to the base better. The base needs to be redesigned entirely to keep the sensor lens within focus distance if cut outs are being removed. Otherwise it will cause tracking issues as seen in several WLMOUSE products like Huan, Ying and G-Wolves HTM Plus

12) A satin clear coat finish.
A satin clear coat on raw Carbon Fiber filled Polymer (as seen in the ULX Competition) will make the mouse look black (except when it is under light), capturing both the minimalistic section of the market that wants a plain black mouse as well as the boisterous section of the market who wants a Forged Carbon Fiber look mouse. 


13) Having colorways which are Pantone matched with popular GMK sets.
I believe this will help break into the lucrative enthusiast keyboard market. Additionally, there may be a mental benefit here to giving a player a clean matching setup. As the old saying goes, when your workspace looks good, you feel good.


14) Implementing hotswap components via claw springs.
The Millmax 3305 is a claw spring that fits the legs of a microswitch as an alternative for soldering. This is an unpatentable way to implement user-hotswappable microswitches and encoders. There is a good chance that this technique could also eliminate the regulatory concerns often caused by exposed pins since the claw clips would cover them.


15) Using a fully solid shell.
A solid shell not only reduces the difficulty of injection molding (since it eliminates isolated and hard to reach areas created by cutouts), but it also minimizes susceptibility to liquids and prevents significantly less dust from getting into the encoder (which is an extremely common failure point for a mouse). A fully solid shell does have the downside of making perspiration on the surfaces slightly more noticeable (since there is less airflow), but this can easily be compensated for by a high-end coating. To further demonstrate the significance of this change, perspiration build up is commonly deemed much more acceptable than the “gunk” (dead skin and hair) build up caused by cutouts or a golfball texture.


16) Implementing a ceramic coating.	Cerakote’s mass production oriented F-Series would eliminate the risk of any color shift since it has excellent chemical and ultraviolet durability, and its film thinness adds virtually no weight (the same cannot be said for the current rubberized coating).	A ceramic coating has been used in mice before, most notably on the scroll wheel of the Zaunkoenig M2K, and it received great consumer reception. When it was applied to the whole mouse by PMM, the surface friction was so high it went viral for its extremely desirable ability to allow you to pick up a mouse with just 2 fingers. A bad coating on your first drop can ruin your first impression and reputation, making the higher cost of a ceramic coating not only well worth it, but disingenuous to not have if youre advertising you’re building the best product possible.It is very important that this is advertised as “baked ceramic coating” versus PMM’s “air dry ceramic coating” since PMM’s ceramic coating, despite being extremely grippy, had durability issues. For marketing we can explain how because ceramics need heat to reach their full strength, there is a reason why a dried clay pot is weaker than a pot put in a kiln, the PMM coating in this analogy is the pot that has not been put in a kiln.Prolonged usage of the current coating has shown its susceptibility to being rubbed off, ceramic coating on the other hand cannot be rubbed off with anything short of something the same hardness as a diamond. Lastly, because Polypropylene has lower surface energy Cerakote is a perfect fit since it relies on how the surface is sanded rather than the material composition for its bond. 

17) Straightening the rear trigger cutouts.
This ensures the fulcrum of the click tensioning mechanism is aligned with where the load will be applied so that none of the input is wasted by being converted to a horizontal force component. The current “smile” style rear trigger cutouts may look more aesthetically pleasing and be more characterized, but since the click tensioning mechanism has to take the shape of the rear trigger cutouts, using a “smile” shape actually causes the click tensioning mechanism to bend inward diagonally (instead of straight up and down). This introduces mechanical latency and harms click feeling, making it of utmost importance that the rear trigger cutouts get reshaped. This is likely why the Viper V2 Pro had angled rear trigger cutouts, but the Viper V3 Pro had them completely straightened after complaints of poor click feelings.


18) Aligning the click tensioning mechanisms with the microswitches.	Right now, the ULX only has one click central click tensioning mechanism, so it’s impossible to even align it to 2 microswitches, but even a lot of mice that do have dual/split click tensioning mechanisms don’t have their click tensioning mechanism aligned with the microswitch. Which is yet another way force can be converted to the horizontal component from the vertical component and further harm click feeling. Aligning the click tensioning mechanisms is only a theoretical (yet physically grounded) suggestion and will need to be tested as it is a novel solution.


19) Angling the charging cable.
	This helps prevent the mouse from running over the cable while it is charging. Angling the cable upwards keeps the cable as far away from the bottom of the mouse as possible, which is especially important for any mouse shape where the chin of the mouse is angled inwards towards the bottom in such a way that it is prone to sucking the cable in. I predict this will measurably increase player performance and consistency while the mouse is charging, reducing the chance the customer will switch to a new mouse after this one dies. Many brands like Lamzu and Pulsar already do this for a reason.


20) Finding a Goldilocks thickness for the cable.
The current cable is much too thick, it makes it impossible for the dongle to sit flat and therefore very easy for the dongle to fall off a desk and onto the floor. When this happens, it often goes unnoticed and destroys wireless latency. The current cable is also so thick that it can cause the mouse to move on its own when the tension in the bent cable is stronger than the weight of the mouse (and this issue would only get worse if further weight savings are implemented). Additionally, the cable cannot be too thin since that will cause slow charging, but surely a middleground can be found. If anything most users would likely rather have slower charging than the absurd thickness they have now. Apple’s cables would be a really good candidate for this. 


23) Implementing web based software.
	No one wants to download unnecessary software anymore, so it is very important that the main solution is web-based. It would also be helpful if the web software was hosted on the main store page domain since it’s easier to remember and access, doing this would also probably help drive repeat sales.
 

26) A film thin bottom plate sticker.
	G-Wolves used to do this a lot on older mice such as the HTS Plus. It was a great way to give a mouse more solid appearance for as little additional weight as possible while also reducing vulnerability to liquids. This sticker would need to be semi-permanent with a warning printed on it to not remove it.


27) Moving the sensor forward.
	One customer dremeled out a more forward slot for the sensor in the ULX PCB and then hand-soldered the sensor to be up more, this demonstrates how fierce the demand is for this feature. Research has also shown that a more forward sensor position also widens the arc of a flick and therefore has a strong correlation towards being beneficial to aim.


28) Implementing POM pads to the left and right of plungers.
	This implementation reduces the grinding which is prominent in Carbon filled Polymers because of their inherent abrasiveness. This also solves the alignment issue on many Carbon Fiber mice, such as the ULX. Lastly, changing the thickness of these pads should theoretically allow for an easy iteration of the tolerances. There was much fanfare from the enthusiast community when Finalmouse announced they would be doing this for the SLX.


29) Adding a divider between the side buttons.
	Implementing this feature increases the strength of the shell in what is normally a structurally weaker area. This also adds a spot to rest your thumb without actuating either side button, but that still has easy access to both of them. This is a heavily requested feature and meshes well with Dual Rail Side buttons. A direct example of the improvement this creates can be seen by feeling the difference between the Viper V2 and Viper V3 Pro for side button intense workloads.


30) Enhancing the scroll wheel texture.
	It would be beneficial for aesthetics, marketing, and tactile sensations to implement a new scroll wheel texture possibly based on a rover’s tread (as those patterns are engineered for longevity and consistency). The look of the scroll wheel is the number one thing that makes a lot of mice with mostly good design aesthetic end up looking cheap, so its very important careful consideration is put into the scroll wheel design. Any RGB, transparent plastic, or shiny plastic on the scroll wheel can usually immediately make an entire mouse look cheap.

(rubber being optional scroll wheel?)


32) Enhanced the top engravings.
	Engravings on the triggers aren’t inherently bad, so long as they aren't in the center of the trigger, since that can cause distracting and unnecessary tactile stimulation. History has shown that engravings are a very important driver of sales since they give a mouse enough character to stand out, they have also become a hallmark of high end collectors mice in recent years. Lastly, they open the door for more expressive colorways.


33) Adding engravings to the side(s).
	This makes the mouse look more sleek and hasn’t been done before (with the exception of the Waizowl Triaina. A more stylish mouse increases player confidence and therefore may even increase performance via a better mindset.


34) Less deep engravings.
	The current depth of the engravings on almost every mouse on the market with engravings so far is likely compromising the overall strength of the mouse. The human eye is very sensitive to a break in a pattern so the depth of the engravings could easily be reduced twofold without impacting visual perception.


35) Serialization to end in 9s.
	Having one less figure in the serialization makes the mouse look more exclusive and makes you feel more special using it, humans also find patterns ending in 9s aesthetically pleasing as commonly shown in price tags.


36) A large laser engraving of the serialization on the right click.
	Laser engraving the 3 digit serial number on right the click makes the serialization perpetually visible and always front of mind to the consumer. It's a very obvious differentiation between units, making each mouse (even ones that are the same colorway) feel unique. Lastly this also incentivizes “super-enthusiasts” to purchase multiple units of each colorway to try to pull a desirable serial number, instead of just one unit of each colorway.


37) Using a smaller sensor lens.
For the newer LGA sensors, Pixart has smaller clip-on lenses that reduce weight. Many high-end mice are still using the large oval skate sensor lens, when it would be much more optimal to just have a lens that only supports a single dot skate.


40) A lower front height.
 	The closer a user’s fingers are to the pad while resting on the triggers, the closer using the mouse will feel to your hand just resting in its natural position which is also the position at which the user will also experience the least strain. Furthermore, this allows for the usage of shorter and lighter encoders.



41) Orientating the hump more towards claw grip.
Claw has become a much more popular grip style in recent years to compensate for this as market knowledgeability of the different grip styles grows. Therefore, it is important that newer shapes have a slightly higher, and slightly further back hump then what would have been more commonplace a few years ago. The difference in shape between the Viper V2 Pro and Viper V3  Pro is an amazing example of other brands capitalizing on the market shift.


44) Miscellaneous shape changes to increase contact area.
	This can be tested and optimized by using a coarse white 3D print of a shape, covering your hands in paint, and then using Photoshop to determine what percentage of the white pixels on the mouse surface were painted. This is a very old school way of testing ergonomics, but a direct correlation has been found between more paint coverage and positive attributes of a shape (such as comfort and performance) by past shape designers.


45) Miscellaneous changes in shape decrease tension and muscle strain.
	Investment into EKG monitors for proper objective data of which shape iterations cause more muscle strain could be very worthwhile. It has the opportunity to considerably speed up shape development. Currently Zowie is the only company to take the Sports Science of shape design seriously enough to do this.


46) Implement flat front trigger cutouts.
The trigger cutouts which the triggers rest on and that are only visible when looking at the front/chin of the mouse head-on need to be perfectly flat and parallel to the ground. This makes additive and subtractive mold modifications to adjust post and pretravel easier.


47) Making the comfort grooves shallow out near the front.
	This is necessary for the front trigger cutouts to be flat and flush, but it also has the added benefit of increasing the amount of surface contact your fingertips have (because of the way it cradles the front of them).


48) Making the shoulder trigger side walls flush to the triggers when actuated.
	There is certainly a happy medium of trigger height offset, the default way will cause the triggers to sit too low when actuated causing perceptible tactile height disparity. Having them artificially raised too high up when not actuated can also cause this issue though.


49) Using one screw per click tensioner.
Far too many mice have 2 screws per trigger click tensioning mechanism, but that exponentially increases how difficult it is for the average consumer to get the click tensioning right compared to one screw, even if the overall click tensioning is less secure with just one screw per mechanism, the ease of maintenance is a worthy tradeoff.

50) Implementing a bridge on the click tensioner.
	The current click tensioning mechanism is just a flat plane, introducing a bridge style mechanism in the shape of an arch with thinner material towards the center will allow us to control where the mechanism actually bends (as well as controlling by how much it bends, which is very important since the proposed material to be used in the triggers is extremely stiff).


51) Using wires instead of ribbon cables.
	Ribbon cables are the most common point of failure in mouse assembly and disassembly (because they are so easy to rip). It is absolutely imperative that small weight efficient wires are used instead of ribbon cables to increase assembly yield rates and decrease the amount of customers that break their product doing maintenance (which will likely subsequently reduce the return rates of the product and the amount of customers who file for an RMA).


52) Move the bottom rim screws so they aren’t under the skates.
	One of the most commonly requested features in a mouse is making sure the screws aren’t under the skates will increase repairability and minimize the downside of repairing your mouse and prevent skate waste. Any company that does this gets a massive amount of consumer favor for being pro-consumer.


53) Converting any separated floor to ceiling struts into fully connected arched ribbing.
	This is vastly more structurally efficient, and it perceptibly increases the shell strength at the cost of very little extra weight gain. Arch bridges (and arches in general) have outstanding force distribution, and that is why they are not only the strongest type of bridge, but also an extremely valuable case study in structural engineering.


54) Mounting the PCB to the top of the shell.
	The screws for the PCB should be inserted from the bottom, and not the top. This makes them easier to access for disassembly and should also offer a moderate reduction to the cost of the tooling/moulding.


55) Implementing vertical struts in the front of the mouse. 
	Due to the front being the least tall area of the mouse, it is structurally the weakest and therefore needs ample reinforcement so it doesn’t detract from the strength of the mouse or become a possible break point. The Lamzu Maya uses these types of supports to great effect (likely because it also has quite a low front). It also does an excellent structural trick of connecting these struts to the trigger underwiring for even more structural efficiency.


56) Hollowing out the plungers in the direction of the user.
	Doing this creates a small echo chamber that amplifies the sound of the click while also causing harmonious reverberation, similar to how a guitar works. The Gilmouse Diamond is one of the only mice on the market to do this, but to great effect.


57) Implementing a more unibody construction.
	The majority of the bottom rim, hump and sides should all be one part. This will increase molding cost, but be worth it in the end by significantly increasing strength, and vastly reducing the amount of crucial tolerance points and panel gaps (these issues only arise when there’s many parts, so eliminating the amount of parts eliminates the issues).


58) Removing the cutout above the scroll wheel dividing the left and right triggers. 
	Doing this will prevent the user from jamming the triggers with their fingers during 1-3-1 grip. The current immediate edge of the trigger is a common spot to rest your finger, but the center is a much less common resting spot. Essentially, removing the double split needed for the cutouts reduces the amount of gaps your fingers can get stuck in from 2 areas to 1 unlikely and further away place. 


59) Making the click tensioner’s fulcrum larger.
	A taller fulcrum means more room to create leverage, which creates lighter clicks with a faster rebound time. This should make the clicks feel much more defined, the click tensioning mechanisms on the Lamzu Atlantis are offset 10mm from the peak of the mouse. I presume this is to give them the ample leverage they need to sound and feel so good. No other mice spend the material to do this, and yet there has to be a reason for this offset.


60) Laser etching texture on the ceramic coating to even further increase grip.
	Allows for quick iteration to find high grip patterns and would also look interesting. Due to it being a final production step it could even be optional for the customer.


61) Using laser engravings instead of physical mold engravings for select decals.
	Such as kanji on the side that would change every colorway, so as to not reduce structural integrity. Also opens the doors for customization without changing the production.


62) Adding “LT” and “RT” decals onto the respective triggers.
	All of the keys on match GMK use a specific font and are labelled, this would try to imitate that. I think it would increase both controller user and GMK user acquisition.


63) Implementing geometric designs on the bottom of the PCB.
	This is important since without a bottom rim the PCB will be constantly visible, so having aesthetic designs on the bottom would add character and show consideration that the mouse is meant to be used with the bottom rim off.


64) Structurally supporting the side buttons with the microswitches and PCB.
	This is the Hitscan Hyperlight implementation that was used to make its side buttons so sturdy and stop them from being pressed in too far.


67) Better tolerancing for the scroll wheel aligner.
 	The scroll wheel has been a constant area of issue for many past releases across many generations of mice and it not only needs better tolerancing, but a complete redesign.


68) Moving the antenna as far forward as possible. 
 	Doing this ensures as little of the mouse as possible is in between the dongle and the antenna. Any amount of material in front the antenna matters when you’re dealing with a shell made out of a composites which have a great propensity to introduce radio impedance.


69) Braiding battery and side button wires.
	Logitech does this with all the GPXs, it increases wire strength. This is pretty self-explanatory with the exception that the braids can’t be too tight or it will increase weight too much (as wires are heavy).


71) Rotating the battery JST socket sideways.
This reduces RMA fees by making it easier for customers to do their own battery replacements and mods, which therefore reduces the downtime of the mouse. This will also likely reduce assembly cost as well, as anything that is easier for the customer to take apart is easier for the assembly line to put together.


72) Putting accessories in an envelope with a trifold display.
An envelope would help reduce unboxing clutter and enable a more premium and curated unboxing experience. Sheets with extra sets of grip tape and skates would be connected to each other and fold out to an elaborate trifold style display that also doubles as a manual on the other side (as a cost effective way to maximize the cost- to-benefit of the color printing).


73) Including screw driver.
This would just be a nice gesture to gain customer gratitude. It would also ensure every customer has the necessary tools to make their own repairs, so not everything has to be sent for RMA. Phillips screwdrivers are also less common in some countries so this could provide the customer a good amount of value in certain circumstances.


74). Adding custom battery cover.
 	The cover would have to be super thin for weight reasons, but all high end mice have the battery covered these days because having the battery exposed makes a mouse look like a prototype. The cover could be made out of laminate satin paper.


75) Using hexagons instead of triangles.
 	Triangles are much better than hexagons for 3D shapes since they form arches when used in a 3 dimensional lattice. Additionally, triangles are also much more stiff than hexagons because they are statically determinate. It is very confusing why the majority of mice on the market use hexagons instead of triangles.


76) Upgrading the sensor and undervolting it.
 	The PAW 3395 can struggle on certain glass pads and therefore severely needs to be upgraded. Additionally, undervolting the sensor could decrease power consumption by up to 40% and make a statement to the customer that we are trying to squeeze out every ounce of performance for them as possible. Consumers have been complaining that the ULX sensor is outdated for over 2 years now. 


77) Upgrading the MCU to an NRF 54LM20A. 
 	An upgrade like this reduces the amount of components needed on the PCB and vastly improves battery life (as well as latency) because of the efficiency of the Nordic 54 Series. In particular, NRF 54LM20A reduces the need for a second and very costly High Speed USB Microcontroller, as well requiring fewer power passives and it does not need a 32khz crystal or external flash memory.




79) Implementing negative latency via SPDT. 
 	After trying negative latency on G-Wolves and EGG mice, many customers can’t go back. So in order to still interest that section of the market, any new release would need to have negative latency in order to reach maximum possible sales. This also should frankly just be done in the pursuit for peak performance as well.


80) Adding dual rail side buttons.
 	This makes the side buttons act more like a Gateron Dual Rail switch, the side buttons would essentially be put on sliding rails instead of a bending mechanism.


82) Entirely hiding the mold vestige gates.
 	No vestige gates can be visible on an enthusiast grade mouse, this is a topic social media has shown enthusiasts are extremely critical of.o


83). Adding holes in the side button PCB as slots for the dual rail mechanism.
 	This reduces molding complexity so the dual rail slots don’t have to be part of the shell, but is only viable in certain PCB orientations.




91) Making the side button PCB reach across the whole mouse.
  	A similar implementation could alternatively be done to reinforce a large area of the left side wall instead (depending on the final orientation of the side button PCB).


92) Placing as many of the components as possible on the top side of the PCB.
 	Doing this would make it harder to damage the components while screwing in the PCB and it would also look more aesthetically pleasing.


93) Implementing thicker walls near the bottom of the mouse.
 	This would be essentially just gradually changing the lattice thickness. It shouldn’t cost anything extra to mold/tool, it just is difficult to CAD (which is why I suspect no one has done it yet despite it being inherently more efficient than a statically thick wall).

94) Connecting the side ribbing to the PCB.
 	The material in the core of a PCB, FR4, is just as strong as Magnesium Alloy, so not using the PCB as a reinforcement leaves a huge amount of efficiency on the table. The weight of the PCB is non-negotiable, so you may as well take advantage of it.


95) Balancing the center of mass lower.

6) Switching the trigger material to Polyphenylene Sulfide CFR Polymer.
Mitsubishi’s Polyphenylene Sulfide CFR has a nanocrystalline structure similar to that of what would typically be found in a metal, this gives it a very “metallic” sound profile. Many past customers miss the crispness of the Starlight clicks, and this metallic sounding plastic is a way to bring this crispness back without introducing the radio frequency impedance and weight drawbacks that typically come along with using metals. Polyphenylene Sulfide CFR, while not quite as light as Polypropylene CFR is still significantly lighter than Magnesium Alloy, making it a clear-cut superseder.

9) Upgrading the screws used.
Phillips slotted combination drive sockets lead to much less slippage during tensioning because Phillips sockets are self-centering and statistically very forgiving on low torque screws. The “combination drive” aspect also allows for a fallback of any size flathead screwdriver in case the Phillips socket somehow strips. The screws used should be brass plated to help reduce the chances of corrosion between the screws and the brass heat set inserts. However, plating only offers a moderate improvement and is something that is best not to waste too much development time on. An aspect of the screws that would actually be worth the development time that would actually be useful is ensuring they are made with a but they also need a Carbon Steel core because magnetism makes assembly much easier. M2 size screws seem to have the best availability and compatibility of parts, so even though they are a little larger than the M1.6 currently used in the ULX, switching to M2 would likely be worth it for the chance that a more specialized screw can be found. Lastly, it is super important that machine screws are used instead of self tapping ones, as machine screws have much better compatibility and longevity with brass heat set threaded inserts.

5) Switching the main shell material to Polypropylene Carbon Filled Polymer.
Polypropylene Carbon Filled Polymer (from Mitsubishi's Pyrofil series) is less dense than ABS while also being at least three times stronger and stiffer. Polypropylene composites should be considerably easier to mold than the Mitsubishi Kyronmax composite used in the current leadership material science mice(because of Polypropylene's exceptional melt flow rate). The inconsistencies in build quality between the same copies of units can likely almost directly be attributed to the poor flow of the material the ULX uses. Additionally, a less dense material allows for the shell to be thicker without necessarily being heavier, reducing the pressure and resistance the liquid composite needs to overcome to fill the mold, further reducing the likelihood of inconsistencies arising. Lastly, this is the same material used in weight-sensitive military applications such as drones and UAVs.
