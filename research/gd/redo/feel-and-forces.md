## Reading log (so nothing is lost)

### Gordon & Morison 1982, Perception & Psychophysics 31(5) 446-450 (full text, Springer open PDF; read twice: verified)
- Stimuli: plano-convex glass lenses masked to a 10 x 20 mm strip (30 and 40 mm in Experiment 3), i.e. convex spherical caps
  with a raised paper edge round them. Convex only.
- Touch: one fingertip of the preferred hand, stroked twice across the strip, about 1 s for the double stroke; forearm
  still, wrist and fingers moving; talcum powder on the finger to cut friction.
- Method: forced choice against a flat blank, 20 presentations per stimulus; threshold from a regression of z-scores on
  height. The paper does not print the percent-correct criterion; Pont et al. 1999 (p. 875) take it as 75 %.
- Table 1: absolute threshold 0.09 mm base-to-peak (SD 0.03) on the 20 mm strip (n = 20 after exclusions).
- Table 2: 20 mm 0.09 mm (SD 0.03), gradient .009 (SD .003); 30 mm 0.13 (SD 0.06), .009 (SD .005); 40 mm 0.18 (SD 0.05),
  .009 (SD .003). "Gradient" = height / half the length. Experiment 4 (staircase, 10 x 40 mm): spherical 0.22 mm (SD 0.10),
  gradient .011; cylindrical 0.25 mm (SD 0.09), .012.
- Older figures the paper quotes: Rubin 1936 .014; Crewdson & Zangwill 1940 .017; Davidson 1972 .013 (blind), .017 (sighted).

### Pont, Kappers & Koenderink 1999, P&P 61(5) 874-894 (full text via SciSpace copy; read twice: verified)
- PVC strips 20 cm x 2 cm, circular arcs, curvature -4 to +4 /m, convex and concave.
- Dynamic (Exp. 3): right index finger under its distal phalanx swept to and fro over windows of 5, 10, 15, 20 cm, two sweeps,
  the forearm moving; flat reference against 14 test strips; threshold = 84 % point; 3 subjects.
  Thresholds 3.3 to 5.3 deg of attitude difference, not changing with length (p. 889-890).
- Static (Exp. 1): three fingers resting on the strip; 2.8 deg with one non-level attitude, 1.6 deg with two (p. 884).
- On Gordon & Morison (p. 875, 890): gradient .009 at 75 % correct = about 2 deg total attitude difference; 2.8 deg at 84 %.
  Their own dynamic thresholds are about twice that; reasons given: bigger strips and forearm scanning.

### Pont, Kappers & Koenderink 1997, P&P 59(8) 1225-1240 (full text, Springer open PDF; read once: partly)
- Static touch, strips 20 cm x 2 cm (8 cm in Exp. 2), -1.8 to +1.8 /m, 3 subjects, 84 % point.
- Along the fingers: 0.35 to 0.55 /m about flat; across the fingers about 1 /m (twice as high).
- Conclusion: the effective stimulus is the total difference of surface attitude over the contact length; thresholds in
  radius grow with contact length. Single-fingerpad thresholds are far higher (they cite Goodwin 1991, about 5 /m).

### Louw, Kappers & Koenderink 2000, Exp Brain Res 132 369-374 (abstract only: partly); 2002 P&P 64 1108-1119 (abstract: partly)
- Gaussian bumps and dents 0.15 mm to 240 mm wide; threshold amplitude 1 um to 8 mm; a power law of the width with exponent
  about 1.3 from 1 mm up; convex = concave. 2002: discrimination thresholds two to four times detection.

### Johnson, Hagberg, Wigaeus Hjelm & Rempel 2000, SJWEH 26(5) 398-405 (full text PDF, read three times)
- 16 office workers (8 men, 8 women), a force-sensing Apple ADB II mouse, a whole working day.
- Results text (p. 402): sides in regular work 0.43 N (SD 0.13) against 0.92 N (SD 0.35) in "simulated use" (the subject
  squeezing as they think they do), P < 0.01, r = 0.16. Verified (three readings agree).
- Table 2, regular work: 0.35 N (SD 0.06) mean and 1.41 N (SD 0.24) peak. The table's text layer is scrambled; the
  column is the button's by this check: the same column's task rows (dragging 0.90, pointing 0.30, text editing 0.63) differ
  from 0.35 by the centres of the confidence intervals printed after them (0.55, 0.05, 0.28), and those intervals are the
  second in each row. Partly verified (layout inferred).
- MVC: sides 79.5 N (SD 21.4), button 52.8 N (SD 16.8) (p. 401). Verified.
- The abstract: the PDF prints "0.6 % (0.35 N) and 0.8 % (0.43 N)" for sides and button; the journal's web page prints
  0.6 %MVC (0.43 N) and 0.8 %MVC (0.35 N). The web page agrees with the results text. The recorded note stands.
- No lifting, no gaming, no fast swipes; a mouse of unknown weight (not given in the paper).

### Derler & Gerhardt 2012, Tribology Letters 45 1-27 (full text, RERO; read twice: verified)
- Abstract: dry skin "greater than 0.2 and typically around 0.5"; wet skin typically above 1.
- Fingers on flat surfaces (Sect. 3.2.1): dry 0.60 +- 0.38 (median 0.45); moist or wet 1.28 +- 0.67 (median 1.25); most
  measured below 5 N.
- Static 10 to 40 % above dynamic in several studies. Dry friction falls with roughness to a minimum near Ra 3 um.

### Dzidek, Adams, Zhang, Johnson, Bochereau & Hayward 2014, EuroHaptics (LNCS 8618) 109-116 (author copy; read twice: verified)
- One subject, index finger, washed and dried, 0.02 to 2 N, sliding 24 mm/s. F = k W^n.
- Smooth glass: n 0.86 -> 0.63, k 0.34 -> 1.21 from first contact to steady state, time constant 18 +- 5 s.
- Smooth polypropylene: n 1.10 -> 0.80, k 0.43 -> 1.08, 16 +- 3 s.
- Rough polypropylene (Rq 6 um, abraded): n 0.98, k 0.53, no change with time.
- Derived: at 0.4 N, mu = k W^(n-1): smooth PP 0.39 at first touch, 1.30 once the skin has sweated against it (about 50 s);
  rough PP 0.54 throughout.

### Shi, Heung, Tang, Tong & Li 2020, Front. Bioeng. Biotechnol. 8 592637 (full text; read once here, matches the record: verified)
- Index MCP, flexion-extension, wrist 0 deg, PIP and DIP splinted, moved 90 -> 0 deg in 10 deg steps with 30 s holds, subject
  relaxed. Stiffness = slope of a double-exponential fit, averaged between an initial angle and 0.7 of the resting angle.
- Healthy (4 men, 24-25 y): 0.0377, 0.0328, 0.0466, 0.0393 N m/rad (standard device). Resting MCP 45, 46, 40, 58 deg.
- Table 4 (their estimates from the older papers): Kuo & Deshpande 2012 about 0.016 to 0.210 (n = 10); Esteki & Mansour 1996
  about 0.025 to 0.058 (n = 3), between 0 deg and the resting angle.

### Milner & Franklin 1998, IEEE TBME 45(11) 1363-1375 (author copy; text read twice, tables not in the text layer: partly)
- Index fingertip, flexion-extension plane only (no side-to-side), 4 mm displacements in 50 ms, force read 130-150 ms later.
- 9.7 N/cm (SD 1.9) along one direction at about 10 N of fingertip force; at 20 % MVC the largest stiffness is two to four
  times the relaxed finger's. The relaxed values are in tables that could not be read.

### Hiramatsu, Kimura, Kadota, Ito & Kinoshita 2015, PLoS ONE 10(9) e0138506 (abstract via DOAJ: partly)
- Precision grip, 17 adults, objects of 6 to 200 g in 14 steps, sandpaper and rayon surfaces. Above 30 g grip force follows
  the load; below 30 g it stops scaling down and the grip-to-load ratio climbs as the object gets lighter.
