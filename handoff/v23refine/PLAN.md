## The story (one paragraph)

The giants each sell a flagship that wins one thing by giving up another: light or long-lasting, stiff or light, fast or
efficient. Theseus is the underdog: no factory, no brand, no budget to out-spend them. So it out-engineers them, one move
at a time, and every move is built on the ones before: the grams one move saves, the next move spends; the stiffness one
move earns, the next move relies on. The gains compound, and the gap to the giants widens with every move, until on every
axis at once nothing beats it.

## The compounding chain (the order of the film)

Each move names what it builds on and what it unlocks. Numbers come from the deck's own sources; check every one.

0. **The bar, and the giants' compromises** (intro): what "best" means, measured (weight as weighed, density not size,
   battery against weight, the targets); every flagship trades one for another (v23: weightgap, ntr_density, ntr_sizewt,
   ntr_densrank, market, targets, flagships, cover). Ends on the underdog's rule: never trade; build so each gain pays for
   the next.
1. **The shape the hand wants** (v23's shape chapter, moved first): sized against real hands, Newtonian analysis, comfort
   grooves, EMG. It fixes the volume everything else must fill without shrinking it (density, from 0).
2. **A light, stiff shell** (shell): carbon-filled PP, specific stiffness, thin walls, hotspots, putting the weight to work,
   triangles and arches, fillets (filletsize was a 10). Unlocks: grams freed at the same stiffness.
3. **Use the weight you already carry** (shell, structure): the board as a woven-composite tie (shlPcbRib, shcCarry,
   shcArms), Poron pads, mass low near the fingers. Builds on 2: stiffer again for zero added grams.
4. **Clicks that the stiff shell makes possible** (clicks): direct drive, the bridge tensioner (an 8), dual rails, flat
   front and rear cutouts, the switch and hot-swap, latency. Builds on 2–3: a stiff top lets the click be direct.
5. **Glide and tracking** (glide): skates and dots, the sensor module, focus, no sag at the lens (builds on 2–3: a shell
   that doesn't flex keeps the lens at its height).
6. **More hours from the same battery** (electronics; Brock 17:08–17:10, V4): move 4's mechanical switches (no optical
   switches drawing power) and newer firmware let a battery the Viper V4 Pro's size (EEMB's 300 mAh against its 306)
   outlast it: 194 h at 1 khz and 43 h at 8 khz, derived before any firmware gains. The smaller battery takes about 1.6 g
   off the whole mouse (27.71 g), which finishes the density moves 2–3 began: the best of any mouse, wired or not (board:
   4 → 6 "No opticals", 2 and 3 → 6 "Density"). Then one board one side, braided wires, the radio.
7. **A finish that weighs nothing** (surface): film-thin coating (no weight, builds on 2), the grip map, no tape.
8. **Built to be opened** (ownership): the inserts and screws that hold the stiff structure also make it repairable.
9. **Close:** the whole chain on one board, every axis at or past the best flagship at once: zero compromises.

## What makes it compound on screen (the persistent visuals)

- **The build board:** a node graph that stays on screen (small, docked) and grows: each move adds its node and draws the
  links to the moves it builds on; the active move is lit. At each act's end it comes forward full-size and shows what the
  move unlocked. By the close every node feeds the others.
- **The scoreboard against the giants:** the running stats (weight, battery at 8 khz, click and motion latency, stiffness)
  beside the best flagship on each axis; each move moves one or two, and the margin visibly widens.
- **The weight ledger:** grams saved and grams spent, so the viewer sees one move paying for the next.
- **Act cards as moves:** "Move 3: use the weight you already carry", with the board showing where it plugs in.
