## 9. The shape study (F65; its own artifact, in neither deck)

**The concept** (F65.1, Nov 7 12:19):

- Gradient descent on the Ninjutso Sora V2.
- Four local minima: the thumb, the index, the middle finger and the pinky. The ring finger rests on the shoulder.
- The pull of each minimum is set from the hand's rest posture in zero gravity.
- It refines existing shapes; it does not invent a novel one. It must still look like a normal mouse.
- Arcs whose depth, length and angle can be set; excellent motion design.

**His rulings, in order:**

1. Trial both grips and both kinds of pocket.
2. One minimum per finger, no more.
3. "Let the numbers decide" the front flare.
4. Long arcs spanning whole sides, never deep notches ("nothing like normal mice").
5. Felt "over the whole slide" of each finger.
6. Grooves measured against the button's slope.
7. The average hand.
8. Trial 3 is the result ("by far my favorite"): the palm lifted, the two sides mirrored. Symmetry is a rule.
9. The thumb is placed from its own base ("the thumb never goes that far forward").
10. The joint angles at rest, each joint shown on its own and self-explanatory, in the film's finger style, with no 3D hand.
11. One whole, natural hand with the fingers together, keeping the angle breakdown (F65.14).
12. A three-quarter view in at least one place, so all five digits read (F65.16).
13. The research redone (F65.16).
14. The average hand's dimensions shown on it, and the film's own nails (F65.18).
15. A right hand, with every finger visible (F65.19).
16. The thumb's resting bends: his yes to "X-ray values" (F65.20) rested on my overstatement. The figure is only described,
    not measured. The knuckle is open: 39° measured with skin markers, about 25° estimated, or 12.5° described. The tip
    joint is 10° (CORRECTION, F65.20).

**The research redo, the hand's half** (RA, finished: research/gd/redo/hand-at-rest.md on ra-research-redo):

1. **The palm-lifted grip has no rule for where it sits along the mouse.** Its fit is nearly as good anywhere from index tip
   z 10 to z 45, and z 30 follows the solver's starting guess.
   - Everything hangs on it: the thumb's z −4, the pinky's z 4, the 28 mm palm gap, both slides, and the 278 mm radius.
   - Moving it 10 mm moves the thumb 13 mm.
2. **The palm-on-the-hump grip rests on an unmeasured 15° wrist tilt.** Mouse users hold the wrist 15 to 30° back. At 20°
   the thumb's slide falls from 16.7 to 1.3 mm.
3. **The thumb:**
   - Its first link between joint centres is 37 mm, not 46.9.
   - Its base sits 56 mm behind the index knuckle, with a second source.
   - The source was mislabelled: it is one specimen from Goislard de Monsabert's group, not Veeger 1997.
4. **A unit slip.** The little finger's knuckle is 13 mm behind the middle's, not 9.4.
5. **Lee & Jung's angles are right, but the reason for choosing them was wrong.** Their "neutral" averages four arm
   positions, with the wrist from 16° back to 24° forward. The doubt is about ±10° at knuckles and middle joints.
6. **NASA's orbit data are thin:** one undefined "finger flexion" per hand, on six crew, on one day.
7. **New measurements of a hand on a mouse:**
   - Rogers 2008 (100 hands): the thumb's tip 14 to 19 mm ahead of the index knuckle. This supports his "never that far
     forward".
   - Oude Hengel 2008: the wrist 31° back on a mouse the Sora's size.
8. **Sizes:** sourced end to end (ANSUR II recomputed, Greiner 1991, Garrett 1970, Buryanov & Kotiuk in the primary).
9. **Cannot be supported:** the finger spread at rest, the palm's 20 mm thickness, and the 5th to 95th percentile spread as
   a spread between people.

Its three proposed changes, none made yet:

1. A stated rule for the lifted grip's place along the mouse, with the range shown.
2. The hump grip's wrist tilt as a measured range, 20 to 30°, rerun.
3. The corrected thumb and knuckle setbacks. Mean hand: the overlap of the thumb's and pinky's paths is 1.8, 6.3 or
   8.9 mm for a knuckle of 39°, 25° or 12.5° (9.0 mm before).
