What it does: the lattice as a bridge (a 62.5 mm strip across the top, 12.5 mm cells, triangles against one row of hexagons
at the same weight) under the same 10 N press ("a firm press: about a 1 kg bag of sugar"); the pointer drags the weight down
along the chart's weight axis (0.48 → 0.24 g): the honeycomb sags, goes red and breaks under the press at 76 % of the
weight while the triangles hold to half; the chart is Brock's "max load" (the press each takes before its peak reaches the
120 MPa break), and the verdict is the triangles' lead growing 8.6× → 17.7× (half the weight still takes 4.5× what the
honeycomb took at full weight). The why is written on the drawings: a bending wall halved takes a quarter of the load, a
stretching rib halved takes half.
